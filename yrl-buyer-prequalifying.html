Pre-Qualifying Buyer Call

15 questions to ask before the first showing

Buyer Tools

Before You Show a Single Home

Call within 24 hours of first contact
No exceptions — qualify every lead
Takes about 10 minutes

These questions help you understand the buyer's motivation, timeline, and readiness. Don't skip them — a qualified buyer saves you time and earns you more. An unqualified buyer costs you gas money and weekends.

 Opening

Hi, , this is with Your Realty Link, and I'm calling because you expressed interest in buying a home. I'd love to help you with that. Before we start looking at homes together, I want to make sure I understand exactly what you're looking for so I don't waste your time. Do you have about 10 minutes?
1

Motivation

Understand their 'why' — it drives every decision

Why are you thinking about buying a home right now? Listen for urgency. Job transfer, growing family, and lease ending are strong motivators. "Just browsing" needs more qualification.
2

Timeline

Determine urgency and timeline

When do you need to be in your new home? If they say "no rush," ask: "If the perfect home came on the market tomorrow, would you be ready to move on it?" Their answer tells you their real timeline.
3

Pre-Approval

Confirm financial readiness

Have you spoken with a lender yet about getting pre-approved? ... That's an important first step. I work with some great local lenders who can get you pre-approved quickly, usually within 24 hours. Would you like me to connect you with one? No pre-approval = not ready to tour homes. Offer to connect them with a lender. This is non-negotiable.
4

Budget

Confirm budget expectations

What monthly payment are you comfortable with, and has your lender told you what price range that puts you in? Monthly payment matters more than purchase price to most buyers. If they haven't talked to a lender, don't assume — connect them first.
5

Down Payment

Understand loan type and cash position

Do you know roughly how much you're planning for a down payment? Are you looking at conventional, FHA, VA, or USDA financing? Down payment affects loan type, which affects what homes qualify. VA and USDA have property requirements. FHA has condition requirements.
6

Current Situation

Understand their current housing situation

Are you currently renting or do you own a home? ... (If own) Would you need to sell your current home before buying? ... Do you have equity in your current home? If they need to sell first, this becomes a sell-then-buy coordination. Note this — it changes the strategy significantly.
7

Location

Define the search area

What cities or neighborhoods are you most interested in? Is there a specific school district that matters to you? How far are you willing to commute to work? Get specific. "Indianapolis area" is too broad. Push for 2–3 target cities or specific neighborhoods.
8

Must-Haves

Identify non-negotiable features

If you could only have three things in your new home, what would they be? ... What about bedrooms and bathrooms — what's the minimum you need? Limit them to 3–5 must-haves. Everything else is a want. Too many must-haves means no home will ever be good enough.
9

Deal-Breakers

Identify disqualifying features

Is there anything that would be an absolute deal-breaker? Something that would make you walk away no matter how great the rest of the house is? Common deal-breakers: HOA, busy road, no garage, specific school districts, flood zone. Knowing these saves showing time.
10

Search History

Gauge where they are in the process

Have you been browsing on Zillow or Realtor.com? Have you toured any homes yet? ... Is there a specific home you've seen online that caught your eye? If they've been looking for months without an agent, ask why. If they've toured with another agent, find out what happened.
11

Other Agents

Check for existing agent relationships

Are you currently working with another agent, or have you signed any agreements with anyone? If they have a signed buyer agreement with another agent, you cannot show them homes. Be direct about this. If they're just casually talking to someone, that's different.
12

Decision Makers

Identify all decision makers

Besides yourself, who else will be involved in making this decision? Will they be joining us for showings? Never show homes without all decision makers present. You'll show the home twice and risk losing the deal when the absent party disagrees.
13

Buyer Compensation

Set the stage for the compensation agreement

Have you heard about the changes in how real estate agents are compensated? ... Starting last year, all buyers are required to sign a written agreement with their agent before touring homes. I'll walk you through exactly how that works when we meet — it's very straightforward and actually protects you. Don't negotiate compensation on this call. Just plant the seed that it exists and that you'll explain it in person. Confidence is key.
14

Expectations

Understand their expectations for service

What matters most to you in a real estate agent? Communication style, availability, expertise in a specific area? I want to make sure I'm the right fit for you.
15

Next Steps

Schedule the buyer consultation

Based on everything you've told me, I think I can really help you find the right home. I'd love to sit down with you — it takes about 30 minutes — to go over the market, explain the buying process, and map out a plan to find your home. What day works best this week? Always push for an in-person consultation before showing homes. This is where you present the buyer consultation, explain the compensation agreement, and get it signed.
