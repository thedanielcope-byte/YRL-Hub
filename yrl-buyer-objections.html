Buyer Tools

The Isolate Technique

Before responding to any objection, isolate it first: "So what you're saying is [restate their concern]. Other than that, is there anything else holding you back?" This ensures you're solving the real problem — not chasing a smokescreen while the actual concern stays hidden.

"I don't want to sign anything before I see homes"

Isolate First

"So what you're saying is you'd feel more comfortable seeing homes before signing an agreement. Other than that, is there anything else holding you back from working together?"

Response
I completely understand that — and honestly, that's the most common question I get. Here's the thing: as of August 2024, every agent in Indiana is required by law to have a written agreement with you before showing homes. It's not something I invented — it's an industry-wide change designed to protect buyers like you.

Think of it this way: would you hire a lawyer without knowing their fee? Or a contractor without a written estimate? This agreement simply puts in writing what I'll do for you and what it costs. No surprises.

And here's what most buyers don't realize — in most transactions, the seller offers concessions that cover your agent's compensation. So you're getting professional representation, and the seller is often paying for it.

Let me walk you through the agreement — it's two pages. I think once you see how simple it is, you'll feel a lot more comfortable.
Stay calm and confident. This is the #1 objection you'll face in 2026. If you fumble this one, you lose the client. Practice it until it's natural.

"I don't want to pay an agent — I can find homes myself online"

Isolate First

"So what you're saying is you feel like you can handle the home search on your own and you're not sure you need an agent. Other than that, is there anything else holding you back?"

Response
You absolutely can search for homes online — and you should! Zillow, Realtor.com, they're great tools. But here's what they can't do for you:

They can't tell you that the house on Oak Street has foundation issues that the photos don't show. They can't negotiate $15,000 off the price because the comparable sales don't support the asking price. They can't review a 40-page purchase agreement to make sure you're protected. And they can't coordinate inspections, appraisals, and title work to make sure you actually get to closing.

Finding the home is maybe 20% of what I do. The other 80% is protecting you — your money, your timeline, and your interests — from contract to closing. That's where the real value is.

And like I mentioned, in most cases the seller offers concessions that cover my compensation. So you're getting all of that for what often costs you nothing out of pocket.
Don't be defensive. Acknowledge that online search is great — then pivot to the 80% they can't see.

"We're already working with another agent"

Isolate First

"So what you're saying is you already have someone helping you. Other than that, is there anything else that would keep you from considering us?"

Opening
That's great — having an agent you trust is important. Just out of curiosity, have you signed a buyer compensation agreement with them?
If they say NO
Then technically you're not committed yet. I'm not here to badmouth anyone — but I'd love the chance to show you what Your Realty Link brings to the table. Sometimes it's worth comparing before you commit. Would you be open to a quick 20-minute meeting?
If they say YES
I respect that completely. A signed agreement means you've made a commitment, and I wouldn't want someone to do that to me either. If things ever change or when that agreement expires, I'd love to be your next call. Can I stay in touch?
If they have a signed agreement, do NOT try to poach them. It's unethical and violates MIBOR rules. Be professional — plant the seed for later.

"Your commission is too high / Another agent said they'd do it for less"

Isolate First

"So what you're saying is the compensation is a concern. Other than that, is there anything else holding you back from working with me?"

Response
I appreciate you bringing that up — compensation is an important conversation. Let me ask you this: if you needed heart surgery, would you choose the cheapest surgeon? Of course not. You'd want the one with the best track record.

Real estate is the same way. The difference between a good agent and a discount agent can be tens of thousands of dollars in your purchase price. I've seen buyers represented by discount agents miss inspection issues, overpay by $20,000, or lose their earnest deposit because someone missed a deadline.

My compensation reflects the level of service, expertise, and negotiation skill I bring to the table. And I earn every dollar of it — because my job is to save you more than I cost you.

Would you rather save $2,000 on commission and overpay $15,000 on the house? Or invest in an agent who negotiates the best possible deal for you?
Never apologize for your compensation. If you don't believe you're worth it, they won't either.

"We want to think about it"

Isolate First

"So what you're saying is you need a little more time before deciding. Other than that, is there anything else that's giving you pause?"

Response
Of course — this is an important decision and you should feel 100% comfortable. Can I ask what specifically you'd like to think about? Is it the agreement itself, or is there something about working together that you're not sure about?
Listen to their answer, then address the specific concern they raise.
After they share their concern
I completely understand. Here's what I'd suggest — homes in your target area are moving fast right now. The average days on market is under two weeks. While you're thinking it over, great homes are coming and going. I don't want you to miss out on the right home because we waited too long to get started.

Why don't we go ahead and sign the agreement today so we're ready to move when the right home comes along? That way you're not scrambling to get paperwork done when a home hits the market tomorrow morning.
If they still hesitate
Tell you what — take tonight to talk it over. I'll follow up tomorrow at a time that works for you. But I want you to know that I'm excited to work with you, and I'm confident I can find you the right home.
"Think about it" usually means they have an unspoken objection. Your job is to find it. Ask questions until the real concern surfaces.

"Why can't the listing agent just show us the home?"

Isolate First

"So what you're saying is you're wondering why you need your own agent when the listing agent is already there. Other than that, is there anything else holding you back?"

Response
Great question. The listing agent can show you the home — but here's what you need to understand: the listing agent works for the seller. Their job is to get the highest price and best terms for the seller, not for you.

That's like going to court and asking the other side's lawyer to represent you too. Would you do that?

When you have your own agent — me — I work exclusively for you. I review the property disclosures to find issues. I pull comparable sales to make sure you're not overpaying. I negotiate repairs after the inspection. I make sure every deadline is met so you don't lose your earnest money.

The listing agent is going to do what's best for their client. I'm going to do what's best for you. That's a big difference when you're spending $300,000 or more on a home.
This is a great objection to get because the answer is so clear-cut. The dual agency / conflict of interest angle resonates with every buyer.
