YOUR REALTY LINK · THE MILLIONAIRE AGENT PATH — Module 4.1: Capacity vs Cul-de-sac Talent

01Every job has two levels

There is the job as it exists today, and the job as it will exist once the business grows. Capacity talent can do both — they have the desire, ability and judgment to grow past the description you wrote. Cul-de-sac talent does today's job well and goes no further.

The tell is in what they hand back. Cul-de-sac talent says “I did what you asked.” Capacity talent says “I did what you asked, plus the thing that was obviously going to be needed next.” One takes the doing off you. The other takes some of the thinking.

Capacity talent

  • Pushes to get answers
  • Knows what they want, or is actively working it out
  • Raises the bar and wants to work with others who do
  • Pushes you
  • Can eventually replace you at some of what you do

Cul-de-sac talent

  • Has to be pushed to want answers
  • Doesn't know what they want and isn't looking
  • May not know where the bar is set
  • Requires you to push them
  • Hands pieces of the job back
Neither is a judgment about the person. It is a judgment about fit against a job that is going to change. Plenty of excellent people are the wrong answer for a role that has to grow.

03What a hire actually costs

A bad hire

  • 3–4 months to interview and hire
  • 3–4 months to train and realise it isn't working
  • 3–4 months of probation before parting ways
  • 9–12 months gone, and you start over

A great hire

  • 3–4 months to interview and hire
  • 3–4 months to train and confirm it's working
  • Then they perform — and you get priorities, mastery, opportunity, time
  • 6–8 months, and you come out energised

Which is why “how little can I pay and still keep them?” is exactly the wrong question. The right one is how much can I afford to pay so I can keep them as long as possible? Talent held accountable to results pays for itself, usually with room to spare.
