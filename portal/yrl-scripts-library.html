Before you use these

  • Commission is negotiable on every transaction. Never quote a "standard" or "typical" rate, and never suggest rates are set by anyone but you and your client.
  • Scrub every call list against the National and Indiana Do Not Call registries before dialing, and follow YRL's calling policy. This applies to circle prospecting, FSBOs and expireds. Ask Janet if you are unsure — the fines are personal.
  • Buyer representation requires a written agreement before you tour a home with anyone. Never describe your representation as free or no-cost.
  • Fair housing. Describe the property and the facts — never the people. Don't answer "what kind of people live here?"; redirect to public data sources and let the buyer draw conclusions.
  • These are starting points, not a scripture. Rehearse them out loud, then make the wording yours.

The Listing Appointment

Roughly 80% of prequalified listing appointments should end in a signed agreement. Most of the ones that don't were lost on price, and most price conversations are lost by softening. Run this in order — the pricing conversation lands far better after they've told you their plans, not before.

1 · Opening — before the tour
Thanks for having me out, [Name]. Before I look at anything or say a word about price, I'd rather hear from you. What's got you thinking about selling? (Let them answer fully. Don't fill the silence.) And where are you hoping to land after this — do you know yet? (Then:) Got it. And is there a date you need to be out by, or is this more "when it's right, it's right"? Last one, then I'll stop asking questions — if everything went perfectly here, what does that look like to you?

Why this order: everything they say here is what you'll price against, market against, and negotiate against. An agent who tours first and asks second ends up guessing.

2 · The walkthrough
Would you walk me through it? Tell me what you love about it — and be honest about what's going to need attention. Nothing surprises me, and anything I find now is something a buyer's inspector won't get to use against us later. (While walking: note condition, updates, and — just as important — what kind of people they are and how much hand-holding they'll want. That belongs in your file.) What have you put into it since you bought?
3 · The team
Let me tell you how we work, because it's a little different. You're not just getting me. You get a team — someone whose whole job is marketing your listing, someone handling the paperwork from contract to close, and me on strategy, pricing and negotiation. Think of it like any specialist practice. You wouldn't want a surgeon who also processed your insurance paperwork and called to remind you about your appointment. You want the specialist doing the specialist work, with good people handling everything else. What that means for you is nothing falls through a crack because one person got busy.
4 · Marketing — the second job
Here's something most sellers never get told. Your house has two jobs. The obvious one is to sell. The second one is to generate buyers — and some of the people who call about your house won't buy yours, they'll buy another one. That's normal, and it's exactly why marketing your home properly matters even in a fast market. So we run the same fourteen-step plan on every listing whether the market is hot or slow: professional photos and video, the MLS done properly, signage, flyers, open houses, email and social to our database, Just Listed to your neighbors, and weekly updates to you on the same day every week. You'll never have to call me to ask what's happening.
5 · Pricing — the moment that matters
Now the part that decides everything. I'm going to tell you what I actually think, not what I think you want to hear. If you wanted someone to agree with a number, that's easy to find, and it costs you money — because a home that sits gets stale, and stale homes sell for less than they would have if they'd been priced right on day one. Here's what's sold nearby and how yours compares. (Walk the comps.) Based on that, I'd recommend we come out at [range]. (Stop talking. Let them react. Do not fill the silence.) What's your reaction to that?

The discipline: say the number, then stop. The agent who keeps talking after the number is negotiating against themselves, and the seller can hear it.

6 · The close
So — the plan is [price], on the market by [date], photos [day], and you'll hear from me every [day] whether there's news or not. Any reason not to get started? (If yes — go to the objection handlers below. If no:) Great. I've got the paperwork with me — let's get it signed and I'll have the photographer booked before I leave your driveway.

Seller Objection Handlers

The same six objections account for nearly every listing you lose. Answer, then ask a question — an answer that ends in a full stop leaves the seller holding the silence, and they'll usually fill it with another objection.

Another agent said they could get more for it.
Response
They might be right — or they might be buying the listing, which is a real thing in this business. An agent tells you a big number, you sign, and three weeks later the price reduction conversation starts. Here's how to tell the difference: ask them to show you the sold comparables that support it. Not active listings — anyone can ask any price. Sold. If they can show you sold homes that justify it, take it. Genuinely. I'd rather you got the most for your home than that I got the listing. Would it help if I walked you through my comps again so you can compare them side by side?
Can you cut your commission?
Response
Everything's negotiable — commission included, and it's always been that way. But let me tell you what you'd actually be buying and what you'd be giving up, because the fee pays for the marketing plan, the team, and the negotiating. The number that decides what you walk away with isn't my fee. It's the sale price and the days on market. An agent who'll drop their fee without a conversation is showing you exactly how they'll handle the negotiation on your house — and that negotiation is worth many times the difference we're discussing. What's the net number you're trying to hit? Let's work backwards from that.

Never say a rate is standard, typical, or set by anyone. Discuss only your own fee, on this transaction.

We want to try it ourselves first.
Response
That's fair, and plenty of people do. Can I tell you the two things that usually decide how it goes? The first is exposure — most buyers are working with an agent, and they're seeing homes through the MLS. The second is that you'd be negotiating directly with a buyer's agent whose job is to get the best deal for their buyer, not for you. Here's my honest offer: go ahead and try it. If it works, I'll be genuinely pleased for you. But give me a call before you sign anything, even if you never list with me — I'll read it for free. And if you're not under contract in [timeframe], let's talk again. Fair?
We want to wait for spring.
Response
That could be the right call — let's look at it properly rather than guessing. The case for spring is more buyers looking. The case against is that you'll have more competition from other sellers at the same time, so you're a smaller fish in a bigger pond. The buyers looking in [current season] tend to be the serious ones. Nobody tours homes in February in Indiana for entertainment. What's actually driving the timing for you — is it the season, or is it something on your end that needs to happen first?

That last question is the important one. Half the time "wait for spring" means "we haven't figured out where we're going," which is a problem you can help solve today.

We need to get [X] out of it — we have to.
Response
I understand, and I want to help you get there. Let's find out whether the market can. Can we work the numbers backwards together? Your payoff, closing costs, and what you need to walk away with. Then we'll see what sale price that requires and whether the comps support it. If they do — brilliant, we're aligned and we go. If they don't, you deserve to know that today rather than in ninety days after two price reductions. And then we can look at the other levers: timing, what we spend getting it ready, or the terms. Shall we do the maths?
We want to think about it.
Response
Of course — it's a big decision and you should think about it. Can I ask what specifically you want to think about? Because if it's the price, or the plan, or whether I'm the right fit, I'd rather answer it now while I'm sitting here than have you wondering about it on Thursday. (Whatever they name is the real objection. Handle that one.)
The pattern: acknowledge without agreeing → give a real reason → hand the decision back with a question. Agents lose listings by defending, not by answering.

The Price Reduction Conversation

Roughly 65% of listings taken should sell. When one isn't selling, the cause is almost always price — and the longer you wait to say so, the harder the conversation gets. Have it early, with data, and have it in person or on the phone. Never by text.

Set it up at the listing appointment
One more thing before we sign, and I'd rather say it now than in a month. If we're [X] days in without an offer, I'm going to call you and tell you what the market is telling us. That's not me giving up — it's me doing the job you're hiring me for. Can we agree now that when I make that call, we'll look at the numbers together with open minds?

This one sentence at the listing appointment makes the reduction conversation dramatically easier later. You've already got permission.

The call
Hi [Name], do you have ten minutes? I want to walk you through where we are — this is the call we talked about when we listed. Here's what's happened. We've had [X] showings in [Y] days. [Z] homes in your area have gone under contract in that time, and none of them were yours. The feedback we're getting is [summarize honestly]. When a home gets showings and no offers, buyers are seeing it and choosing something else — and that is nearly always a price conversation, not a marketing one. (Pause. Let them respond.) Here's what I'd recommend: [new price]. That puts us [below/at] [comparable], and it brings in the buyers currently searching under [threshold] — which is a group that literally cannot see your home right now. How do you feel about that?
If they say "let's give it a bit longer"
We can. I want to be straight with you about the cost of that. Homes get the most attention in their first two or three weeks. We've spent that. Every additional week, the listing gets less traffic and buyers start asking what's wrong with it — even when the answer is nothing. Waiting doesn't hold the price. It usually means a bigger reduction later, from a weaker position. What if we did this: [new price] now, and if we're under contract in two weeks you'll never think about it again. Deal?
Bring the data to the call. Showings, days on market, what's gone under contract nearby, and the exact search threshold your price is sitting just above. A reduction conversation with numbers is a consultation. Without them, it's just an opinion the seller can decline.

Circle Prospecting & Neighborhood Calls

Calling the neighbors around a listing, a just-sold, or an open house. It works because you have an actual reason to call — you are not cold, you are local and specific.

Do Not Call — read this first

  • Scrub every list against the National DNC Registry and Indiana's Telephone Privacy list before you dial. Indiana's rules are stricter than the federal ones.
  • Follow YRL's calling policy and keep your own do-not-call notes. If someone asks not to be called again, record it immediately and honor it permanently.
  • If you are not certain a number is safe to call, mail or door-knock instead. Confirm with Janet Giles-Schultz (317-997-7404).
Just Listed
Hi, is this [Name]? This is [Your Name] with Your Realty Link here in [town]. Did I catch you at a bad time? (If fine:) I'll be quick. We've just listed the home at [address], right around the corner from you, and I'm calling the neighbors before it goes out more widely — partly as a courtesy, and partly because neighbors often know exactly who ought to move in. Do you know anyone who's been talking about getting into the neighborhood? (Then, regardless of the answer:) Appreciate it. And while I've got you — have you given any thought to what your own place might be worth these days? I'm happy to put something together for you, no strings.
Just Sold
Hi [Name], it's [Your Name] with Your Realty Link. Did I catch you at a bad time? I'm calling because we just closed on [address] down the street, and people nearby usually want to know what it actually sold for rather than what the internet guesses. Would you like me to send you the number and what it means for your own home's value? (If yes — get the email or address, and enter them into the 8×8.)

The just-sold call is the strongest of the three. You are offering genuinely useful information that is hard to get elsewhere, and everyone on that street is curious.

Open house invitation
Hi [Name], [Your Name] with Your Realty Link — did I catch you at a bad time? We're holding [address] open this [day] from [time], and I wanted to invite the neighbors first. Two reasons. You get a look at what a home on your street is worth right now — and if you know someone who'd love to be your neighbor, this is their chance. Any chance you'll stop by?
Every call ends the same way: get a name and a way to reach them, and put them into the 8×8 the same week. A call that produces a nice chat and no CRM entry produced nothing. Build your 8×8 →

Voicemails

Most voicemails fail for the same reason: they ask for nothing and give the person no reason to move. Under twenty seconds, one specific reason, one clear next step, and your number said twice — slowly.

New internet lead — first attempt
Hi [Name], it's [Your Name] with Your Realty Link — you were looking at [property/area] on our site. I've got a couple of others that aren't showing up publicly yet that I think fit what you were looking at. Give me a call and I'll send them over. [phone]. That's [phone]. Talk soon.

Speed beats polish. Response time moves conversion more than anything you say. Call within minutes if you can.

Second attempt — same day
Hi [Name], [Your Name] again with Your Realty Link. Tried you earlier. I'll send what I've got to [email] so you have it either way — no need to call me back for that. But if you'd rather just talk it through, I'm on [phone]. [phone].
Past client check-in
Hey [Name], it's [Your Name] — no reason at all, just thinking about you. I was pulling numbers for [their neighborhood] this week and yours came up. You'd be surprised what it's doing. Give me a shout if you're curious. [phone]. Hope the family's well.
Sphere — the 8×8 week 4 call
Hi [Name], [Your Name] with Your Realty Link. Sorry to miss you. I sent you the market update for [area] last week — just wanted to make sure it landed and see if you had any questions. And if you happen to know anyone thinking about buying or selling, I'd love it if you'd pass my name along. [phone]. Take care.
Say your number twice, slowly, at the end. It is the single most common reason a voicemail gets no callback — they were reaching for a pen and you'd already hung up.

Asking for a Referral

Most of your sphere does not know you work by referral. Telling them is most of the job. Educate → ask for help → reward the act of referring, not just the ones that close — rewarding only closed referrals teaches people to screen for you, which is not their job.

The everyday version
You know most of my business comes from people I already know rather than advertising — that's the way I like it. So if anyone you know is even starting to think about moving, I'd love it if you'd pass my name along, or send me theirs. No pressure on them at all — I'm happy just to answer questions.
At the closing table
Congratulations — genuinely, this was a good one. Can I ask you something? If someone you care about was in the position you were in three months ago, would you feel comfortable putting them in touch with me? (Wait for the answer. It matters.) That means a lot. I'll keep in touch — and it won't just be at Christmas.

Ask at closing, while the goodwill is at its peak. Most agents never ask at all, and then wonder why the referrals don't come.

To an allied resource — lender, title, contractor
Hi [Name] — [Your Name] at Your Realty Link. I'm calling because I'd rather send my clients to people I actually trust, and you've come up. I've got buyers and sellers who need [what they do] pretty regularly. Can I start sending them your way? (Then, and only then:) And if you ever have a client who mentions buying or selling, I'd be glad to look after them for you. Either way — I'll be sending you names.

Give first. Offer the referral before you ask for one. It changes the conversation completely, and they remember it.

When a referral arrives — same day
Hi [Name] — [referral's name] just called me and said you'd passed my name along. Thank you. Genuinely. I know you're putting your own name on the line when you do that, and I don't take it lightly. I'll look after them and I'll keep you posted.

This is the one most agents skip. Thank the source before you know whether the referral turns into anything — that is what makes it happen again.
