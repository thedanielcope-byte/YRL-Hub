Before you mail

  • Fair housing. Market the home and the facts — never the people. Don't describe a neighborhood by who lives there, don't use "safe", "good schools" as a proxy, and don't tailor a mailing to or away from any protected class. Point people at public data and let them decide.
  • Expired listings: the home must genuinely be off-market before you solicit. Check the MLS status the morning you mail, not the week before.
  • Do Not Call applies to phone follow-up on FSBO and expired mailers. Scrub against the national and Indiana lists first, and follow YRL's calling policy.
  • Never quote a commission rate or suggest one is standard. Never describe buyer representation as free.
  • Ranges, not invented numbers. If you don't have the MIBOR figure in front of you, write "the mid-$200s".
  • Include your name, that you're a licensed agent, and Your Realty Link on anything that goes in a mailbox.

Sphere of Influence Letter

The letter that tells everyone you know that you sell real estate — and that you work by referral. Most agents never send it, then wonder why their friends used someone else. Send it once properly, then follow with the 33 Touch.

New agent — the announcement
Dear [First Name], I wanted you to hear this from me rather than see it on Facebook: I've joined Your Realty Link and I'm now selling real estate here in Central Indiana. I'm not writing to sell you a house. You're not moving, and I know that. I'm writing because in this business almost everything comes from people already knowing what you do. So — now you know. If you ever have a question about the market, what your place might be worth, or whether now's a sensible time to do something, ask me. Even if you never buy or sell a thing through me, I'd rather you got a straight answer from someone you know than a guess from a website. And if someone you care about mentions moving, I'd be grateful if you passed my name along. That's it. That's the whole letter. Warmly, [Your Name] Your Realty Link [phone] · [email] P.S. I'll send a short market note now and then — genuinely useful, never pushy. Tell me to stop any time and I will.

Handwrite the envelope and use a real stamp. A hand-addressed envelope gets opened; a windowed one gets binned.

Experienced agent — the re-introduction
Dear [First Name], It occurred to me recently that I've been doing this [X] years and I've never actually told you how my business works. Nearly all of it comes from referrals and people I already know. I don't buy leads and I don't cold-call strangers — I'd rather look after fewer people properly. Which means when someone you know is thinking about moving, and you think of me, that's not an imposition. That's the entire business. So if it ever comes up — a friend at work, a neighbor, your brother-in-law who's been "thinking about it" for two years — send them my way, or send me their name. I'll look after them the way I'd want someone to look after you. And if there's anything you're wondering about your own place, just ask. Warmly, [Your Name] Your Realty Link [phone] · [email]

Geographic Farming Postcards

Twelve pieces a year to the same addresses. Roughly every 50 households mailed 12 times produces about one closing. The copy below is short on purpose — a postcard is read in about three seconds, standing over a bin.

Just Sold — the workhorse
FRONT Just sold on [Street]. BACK [Address] · [beds] bed, [baths] bath · sold in [X] days Curious what that means for your home? The online estimates can't see your kitchen. I can. Call or text me and I'll put together a real number for your place — no cost, no obligation, no follow-up campaign unless you want one. [Your Name] · Your Realty Link [phone] · yourrealtylink.com

The most effective of the twelve. Everyone on the street wants to know what the neighbor's place went for.

Market snapshot
FRONT [Neighborhood] — [Month] BACK [X] homes sold · mostly in the [range] · averaging [X] days on the market Whether you're moving this year or in ten, it's worth knowing what's happening on your own street. Full write-up: yourrealtylink.com/market-updates [Your Name] · Your Realty Link [phone]
Community events — the fridge piece
FRONT What's on in [Town] this [season] BACK [Event] — [date], [location] [Event] — [date], [location] [Event] — [date], [location] [Event] — [date], [location] Stick this on the fridge. Brought to you by [Your Name] at Your Realty Link — because we live here too. [phone] · yourrealtylink.com

The sleeper of the twelve. It's the one people keep, which means your name sits on their fridge for three months. Your Realty Link already has curated event listings for 62 Central Indiana cities — pull from there.

Sell now or wait?
FRONT Sell now, or wait? BACK It's the question I get most, and the honest answer is: it depends on your street, not on the headlines. What's true right now in [neighborhood] is [one genuine sentence]. If you want a straight answer about your specific home rather than a national statistic, call me. Takes ten minutes and costs nothing. [Your Name] · Your Realty Link [phone] · yourrealtylink.com
Design all twelve in January and schedule them at once. A farm year planned in one sitting takes an afternoon; a farm planned month by month quietly stops in March. Plan your 12 Direct year →

Community Newsletter

A page or two, quarterly, to a farm or your past clients. The rule that makes it work: roughly 80% about the place, 20% about real estate. Reverse that and it goes straight in the bin.

Full structure — swap in your content
[TOWN] QUARTERLY [Season] [Year] · from [Your Name] at Your Realty Link — WHAT'S ON [Event] — [date, location, one line of detail] [Event] — [date, location, one line of detail] [Event] — [date, location, one line of detail] — WORTH KNOWING [One genuinely local item — a road closure, a new park, a business opening, something the town is arguing about. Two or three sentences. This is the part people actually read.] — A LOCAL WE LIKE [Name of a business], [street] [Two or three sentences about why. No payment involved, no arrangement — just a place you actually go. Say so.] — THE MARKET, BRIEFLY [X] homes sold in [area] last quarter, mostly in the [range], averaging [X] days on the market. What that means in plain terms: [one honest sentence] Full numbers any time at yourrealtylink.com/market-updates — FROM ME [Three or four sentences. Something human. A house that was hard and worked out, something you learned, something happening with your family. This is the reason people open the next one.] If you ever want a straight answer about your own place — what it's worth, whether a project is worth doing, whether now is sensible — just call. No obligation and no follow-up campaign. [Your Name] Your Realty Link · [phone] 2302 E Southport Rd, Indianapolis IN 46227
The "From me" section is why it works. Anyone can print market statistics. Nobody else can be you. If you cut anything for space, cut the market section — not that one.

Door-Knock Leave-Behind

What you hand over, or leave when nobody answers — which is most of the time. It has to work without you standing there to explain it.

Nobody home — the leave-behind
Sorry I missed you. I'm [Your Name] with Your Realty Link, and I was going door to door on [street] today for a straightforward reason: a home near here just [sold / come on the market], and neighbors usually want to know what that means for them. [Address] · [beds] bed, [baths] bath · [sold in X days / listed at range] If you'd like a proper read on what your own home is worth — the kind that accounts for what you've actually done to it — call or text me. It's free, it takes me about twenty minutes, and I won't pester you afterwards. And if you know someone who'd love to live on this street, I'd love to hear about it. [Your Name] Your Realty Link [phone] · [email] yourrealtylink.com

Never put anything inside a mailbox — that's a federal offense. Door handle, screen door, or wedged in the frame.

The doorstep script
(Step back from the door after knocking. Give them space.) Hi — I'm [Your Name], I'm a realtor with Your Realty Link, and I'm just going along [street] today. I promise I'll be quick. [Address] just [sold / come on the market] — [one specific detail]. I'm letting the neighbors know before it goes any further. Do you know anyone who's been wanting to get onto this street? (Then, whatever they say:) No problem at all. I'll leave you this — it's got the details and my number on it. And if you're ever curious what your own place is worth, that call's free and there's no catch. Thanks for your time. (Leave. Do not linger. The whole thing should take under a minute.)
Brevity is the tactic. The agent who keeps talking on a doorstep is the one who doesn't get invited back. Say your piece, hand over the card, thank them, go.

For Sale By Owner Letter

FSBOs get contacted by every agent in the county, all saying the same thing. The one that works offers help without asking for the listing — because by the time they need you, they've usually stopped answering everyone else.

First letter — week 1
Dear [Name or "Homeowner"], I saw your home on [street] is for sale by owner. This isn't a letter asking you to list with me. You've decided to sell it yourself, and plenty of people do that successfully. It's an offer of help, with nothing attached. If you'd find any of these useful, just ask and they're yours: · The current sold comparables near you, so you know your price is right · The Indiana disclosure forms you're legally required to provide · A plain-English list of what has to happen between accepting an offer and closing I'd rather you had these and sold it yourself than watched you get caught out by something avoidable. One thing worth knowing: buyers working with an agent will have someone negotiating hard on their behalf. If you get an offer and want a second pair of eyes on it before you sign, call me. I'll read it for free, and I'll tell you honestly if it's a good deal — even though that means you won't need me. [Your Name] Your Realty Link [phone] · [email] P.S. If it hasn't sold in a few weeks and you'd like to talk about why, I'm easy to reach. No pressure either way.
Follow-up — week 3 or 4
Dear [Name], Just checking in on [address]. If it's under contract — genuinely, well done. Ignore this. If it isn't yet, here's what I see most often at this point, and it's almost never the house: Not enough of the right people have seen it. Most buyers are working with an agent and searching the MLS. If they're not seeing it, they can't buy it. The price is slightly above a search threshold. Being at [X] when a lot of buyers stop their search at [X minus a little] makes you invisible to people who could easily afford your home. The photos. Almost everyone sees the pictures before the house. That's the real first showing. Happy to give you my honest read on which one it is — for free, and with no expectation. If you decide to keep going alone afterwards, that's completely fine. [Your Name] Your Realty Link · [phone]
Give first, three or four times, before you ever ask. The agents who pitch on day one get ignored. The one who was useful in week one gets the call in week six. And check DNC before you phone — a letter is always safe, a call may not be.

Expired Listing Letter

They're frustrated, and they've just been let down by someone in your job. Lead with the disappointment, not with your marketing plan. Confirm the listing is genuinely off-market before you send anything.

First letter — within days of expiry
Dear [Name], I noticed your home on [street] came off the market recently without selling. That's a frustrating outcome, and I'm sorry. You did the work of keeping it show-ready, you rearranged your life around showings, and it didn't happen. That's genuinely disappointing and I don't want to gloss over it. I'm not writing to tell you your last agent did it wrong. I don't know what happened, and it's rarely as simple as it looks from outside. What I would say is this: a home that doesn't sell almost always comes down to one of three things — how it was priced, how it was marketed, or how it showed. Usually one of them, occasionally two. And it's genuinely fixable once you know which. If you'd like, I'll tell you what I think went on. Free, no obligation, and I'll be honest even if the answer is "your agent did everything right and the market was against you." You can decide afterwards whether you want to try again, wait, or stay put. Any of those is a reasonable choice. [Your Name] Your Realty Link [phone] · [email] P.S. If you've already relisted with someone, please ignore this and I wish you a quick sale.

That P.S. matters. If they've relisted, soliciting them is a problem — and offering the courtesy up front makes the rest of the letter read as decent rather than opportunistic.

Follow-up — two weeks later
Dear [Name], Following up on my note about [address]. I had a proper look at how it was marketed and how it sat against the competition. Three things stood out, and I've written them down for you. You can have that whether or not we ever speak again — reply, call, or text and I'll send it over. I'll say plainly: I would like to sell your home. But I'd rather you had an honest read on what happened than that I chased you about it. If you decide to wait a year, or to try again with someone else entirely, you'll still have the information. [Your Name] Your Realty Link · [phone]
Check the MLS status the morning you mail. If it's been relisted, take them off the list. Soliciting another agent's active listing is both a licensing issue and the fastest way to a reputation you don't want in a market this size.
