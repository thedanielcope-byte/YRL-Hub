Before you send

  • Every marketing email needs an unsubscribe link and a real physical postal address — Your Realty Link, 2302 E Southport Rd, Indianapolis IN 46227. That's CAN-SPAM, and it applies to a newsletter, not to a one-to-one reply.
  • Honor unsubscribes within 10 business days and never re-add someone who opted out to a new list.
  • Never quote a commission rate or imply one is standard. Commission is negotiable on every transaction.
  • Never describe buyer representation as free. A written buyer agreement is required before touring.
  • Use ranges, never invented figures. If you don't have the real MIBOR number in front of you, say "the mid-$200s," not a precise median you guessed.
  • Fair housing: describe homes and facts, never the people who live somewhere.

Post-Closing Thank You

Sent within 24 hours of closing, while the goodwill is at its highest. This is also the single best moment to ask for a review and a referral — and most agents let it pass.

Buyer — day of closing
Subject: Congratulations 🎉
Hi [First Name], You own a house. Congratulations — you earned this one. A few things for the file: · Closing documents are attached, and your title company will send the recorded deed in a few weeks. · Change the locks. Always. First weekend. · Find the water shut-off before you need to find the water shut-off. · Keep every receipt for improvements — it matters when you eventually sell. If you need a good [plumber/electrician/HVAC person], ask me. I keep a list of people I'd use at my own house. And I'm not disappearing now that we've closed. If something breaks, if you're wondering whether a project is worth doing, or if you just want to know what the place is worth in a few years — call me. Enjoy the first night in it. [Your Name] Your Realty Link [phone] · yourrealtylink.com
The review + referral ask — 3 to 5 days later
Subject: One small favor
Hi [First Name], Hope the unpacking is going better than mine ever does. Can I ask a favor? If the experience was a good one, a quick review makes a real difference to a small brokerage like ours — it's how most people decide who to trust. [Google review link] Two lines is plenty. And if anything wasn't right, tell me instead of the internet — I'd genuinely rather hear it and fix it. One more thing. Most of my work comes from people I already know rather than advertising, which is how I like it. So if someone you care about is thinking about moving, I'd love it if you passed my name along. Thanks again for trusting me with this one. [Your Name] Your Realty Link · [phone]

Separate the two emails. Congratulations and a favor in the same message makes the congratulations feel like a set-up. Give it a few days.

Seller — day of closing
Subject: Sold — and thank you
Hi [First Name], Closed. [address] is officially someone else's story now. Thank you for trusting us with it. [One genuine specific — a hard moment you got through, something they did well, something about the house.] Keep your closing statement somewhere safe — your accountant will want it. If there's anything at all I can do as you settle into [new place / next chapter], say the word. And if you need an agent in [new city], let me find you a good one — I'd rather point you to someone I've vetted than let you take a chance. [Your Name] Your Realty Link · [phone]
Add them to your 33 Touch the same day. A past client who hears from you 33 times a year is worth roughly one referral and one repeat sale every six years — a client you never contact again is worth nothing. That gap is most of the difference between a career and a job.

Seasonal & Holiday

Part of your 33 Touch. The rule: be useful or be warm — never both plus a sales pitch. A seasonal email that pivots into "and by the way, is it time to sell?" undoes itself.

Spring — home maintenance

Hi [First Name], Now that Indiana has decided it's spring, five things worth half a Saturday: 1. Clear the gutters — winter fills them and summer storms find out. 2. Service the AC before the first ninety-degree week, not during it. 3. Walk the foundation and look for new cracks from the freeze-thaw. 4. Check the sump pump actually runs. Pour a bucket in and watch. 5. Reseal the deck if water stops beading on it. None of this is exciting. All of it is cheaper than the alternative. Need someone good for any of it? I keep a list. [Your Name] Your Realty Link · [phone]

Get the furnace looked at now — the good techs are booked solid the week it turns. 3. Reverse the ceiling fans. Genuinely helps. 4. Check the water heater. If it's over ten years old, start planning rather than reacting. 5. Find the roof leak while it's still dry enough to fix. If you need a name for any of these, just ask. [Your Name] Your Realty Link · [phone]

Anniversary & Birthday

Three of your 33 touches — two birthdays and the home purchase anniversary. They only work if the dates are actually in your CRM, which for most agents they are not. Fifteen minutes of data entry buys you three touches a year, forever.

Home purchase anniversary
Subject: [X] years on [street]
Hi [First Name], [X] years ago today you got the keys to [address]. I remember [a genuine specific — the inspection drama, the appraisal, the day they saw it]. Hope it's been good to you. No agenda — just marking the day. Though if you're curious what it's worth now, it's changed a fair bit since then. Say the word. [Your Name] Your Realty Link · [phone]

The genuine specific is what makes this work. Without it you've sent a mail-merge and everyone can tell. Write one line in the CRM at closing about something memorable — you will not remember it in three years.

Birthday
