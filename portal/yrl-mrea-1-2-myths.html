01Where fear actually comes from

Most of what limits an agent isn't skill or market — it's a belief that was never tested. A belief built on something true produces good decisions. A belief built on a myth produces a career-sized limitation that feels like common sense.

Below are the six that show up most often. Tick the ones you've caught yourself believing. Honest ticking is the whole exercise — nobody but you sees this page.

02The six

Myth

“I can't do it.”

Truth

Until you try, you have no data. You do not know your own ceiling — nobody does. Deciding in advance that a number is beyond you is a decision made with no information at all. Ask yourself whether you'd teach a child to think that way about themselves.

Myth

“It can't be done in my market.”

Truth

It can — you may need a different approach. What that sentence usually means is “I haven't found the way to make it work here yet.” Central Indiana is not exempt from arithmetic. And the first agent in a market to stop believing this has an advantage over everyone who still does.

Myth

“It would take too much time and effort — I'd lose my freedom.”

Truth

Hours are not the deciding factor. You can work fifty hours and earn fifty thousand, or work fifty hours and earn far more — the difference is what fills the hours and how much of it is leveraged. The highest producers interviewed for this book generally had more freedom, not less, because leverage bought their time back.

Myth

“It's too risky. I'll lose money.”

Truth

Risk is proportional to how loosely you hold spending accountable. Add a cost, then require it to produce a matching result before you add the next one. Spending stops being a gamble the moment it has to report back. That discipline has a name — Red Light, Green Light — and it's Module 2.5.

Myth

“My clients will only work with me — only I can deliver quality service.”

Truth

Clients are loyal to the standard you represent, not to your calendar. And any standard you can write down, someone else can meet. A trained team of specialists usually delivers a more consistent experience than one person doing every job at every hour. Nobody wants a surgeon who also processes their insurance paperwork.

Myth

“Setting a goal I don't fully hit is a negative thing.”

Truth

Setting a goal and never going after it is the negative thing. Small goals are reached and then you coast; big goals drag you past the small ones on the way. Aim high — at night you always underestimate the distance to the target.
