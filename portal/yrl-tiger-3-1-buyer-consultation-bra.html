YOUR REALTY LINK · TIGER TRAINING — Module 3.1: Buyer Consultation & Buyer Rep Agreement

01Why consult before you show

Driving a stranger to houses is the amateur move. A buyer consultation lets you understand their needs, confirm they're financially ready, agree on how you'll work together, and get committed — so you're not wasting weekends on someone who buys with another agent.

The consultation is where you turn a lead into a client. Do it first, every time.

02The Buyer Representation Agreement — required

As of the NAR settlement rules (in effect since August 2024), you must have a signed written buyer agreement in place before touring homes with a buyer. It spells out the services you provide and how you're compensated.

Be clear and honest: buyer representation is a valuable professional service — it is not automatically free. Your compensation is negotiable, and it may be paid by the seller/listing side, by the buyer, or a combination, depending on the deal.

Use Your Realty Link's current Buyer Representation Agreement and compensation language, and confirm the details and any required addenda with Janet Giles-Schultz before you sit down with a buyer. Don't wing the paperwork.

05The compensation conversation

Be transparent and confident. Practice it below.

How you get paid — buyer script

You: Let's talk about how I get paid so there are no surprises. My representation is a professional service with real value — negotiating, protecting your interests, and getting you to the closing table.

In many sales the seller's side offers compensation to the buyer's agent, which can cover my fee. When it doesn't fully, we'll have already agreed on how that's handled — it's all spelled out in our agreement, up front.

(Walk through the YRL Buyer Representation Agreement together and answer questions honestly.)
