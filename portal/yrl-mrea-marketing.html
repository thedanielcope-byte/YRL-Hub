The Touch System

Three programmes, two audiences, one database

Everyone you could do business with is either someone who knows you (Met) or someone you have chosen to market to who doesn’t yet (Haven’t Met). New contacts run the 8×8, then graduate into the 33 Touch forever. Your farm runs 12 Direct. That’s the whole machine.

8×8 — new contacts

8 in 8

Eight touches in eight consecutive weeks. The observed minimum to be remembered. Weeks 4 and 8 are phone calls and stay human.

33 Touch — your Met database

12 : 2

Every twelve people touched 33 times a year produce about two closings — one repeat, one referral.

12 Direct — your farm

50 : 1

Every fifty people mailed twelve times a year produce about one closing. Costs more, but scales without your hours.

The rule that makes all three work: every single touch carries a reminder that you work by referral and instructions on how to send you one. A touch that doesn’t ask is a greeting card.

The Listing Engine

Print one per listing — and run it even when the house sells in four days

A listing has two jobs. Selling the house is the first. Producing the next buyer and the next seller is the second, and it is the one that gets skipped in a fast market. One well-marketed listing should generate about one closed buyer on its own.

Harvest the leads — the half that gets skipped

The test: at every closing, ask how many leads this listing produced. If the answer is none, the second job didn’t happen — and that is worth more than the commission you just collected.

The Referral Loop

Reward the act of referring, not the closing

Rewarding only referrals that close quietly trains your sphere to screen people before sending them. That is not their job. Reward the act — every time, immediately — and treat the source better than the referral.

1 · The call comes in

Same day. Text within the hour, handwritten note in the post. Before you know whether it’s a real deal.

2 · You meet them

Call the source. “Met them, here’s how it went, thank you.” Closing the loop is most of the reward.

3 · Under contract

Something small and local. Coffee, a restaurant card.

4 · It closes

A proper gift and a note — and a mention in the newsletter, with permission.

Check first: confirm with Janet Giles-Schultz (317-997-7404) what your brokerage and Indiana licensing rules allow before offering anything of value for a referral — particularly to anyone licensed or in a related industry.

The Week-7 Giveaway

Eight options — pick one, order two versions

Week 7 of the 8×8 is a physical object. The test is usable, not throwaway — long shelf life, and your name and number visible while it's actually being used. A fridge magnet beats a branded pen by years. Ballpark costs only; get real quotes, prices move a lot with quantity.

ItemWhy it worksBallpark ea.
Local numbers magnet ★ Township trustee, utilities, trash day, school corporation, non-emergency police — built per town. Goes on the fridge and stays for years.$0.20–0.50
School sports schedule magnet ★ Southport Cardinals, Shelbyville Golden Bears. Parents in a farm keep these all season and talk about them.$0.25–0.60
Indy Indians schedule magnet Victory Field is a genuinely local, genuinely liked thing — and a summer schedule with no political edge to it. Broad appeal across the whole metro.$0.25–0.60
Potato chip clips Lives in the kitchen drawer and gets used weekly for years. Cheap, unusual enough to be remembered, and nobody throws one away.$0.30–0.80
Trash & recycling day magnet Hyper-specific per city. Referred to weekly, which is more than almost anything else on this list.$0.20–0.50
Magnetic notepad Used daily — your name is in their hand with every grocery list.$0.75–1.50
Ice scraper Indiana. Used every morning from November to March, and kept in the car where it's seen constantly.$1–2
Colts / Pacers schedule magnet High keep rate, but it expires each season — so it's a yearly reorder rather than a one-off.$0.25–0.60
The recommendation: the local numbers magnet, printed in two versions — one Southport, one Shelbyville/Fairland. Highest utility, genuinely local rather than generic branding, and it doubles as the farm's calling card. If you want the one that gets talked about rather than just kept, make it the school sports schedule. Chip clips are the best non-magnet option and the one people are most likely to mention to someone else.
